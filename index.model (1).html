Solow Growth Model

About the model

Y = A√K    I = sY    D = δK    ΔK = sY − δK

Y = A√K says that output Y is produced from capital K and technology A. Because of the square root, each extra unit of capital adds less output than the previous one (diminishing returns). Capital increases when investment sY is larger than depreciation δK, and decreases when it is smaller. The economy settles at the steady state K*, where sY = δK.

s: savings rate. The share of output that is saved and invested. A higher s raises investment, so capital increases and K* is higher.

δ: depreciation rate. The share of capital that wears out each period. A higher δ raises depreciation, so capital decreases and K* is lower.

A: technology level. Output produced per unit of √K. A higher A raises output and investment, so capital increases and K* and Y* are higher.
